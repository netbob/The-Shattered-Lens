# Act I: The Logic Loop

## Chapter 1: The Ephemeris Error

### Module 1E: The Overpressure Front

The kinetic energy of the primary fragment of Astraea-9—the six-mile-wide core of tightly bound carbonaceous iron and high-pressure methane ice clusters—did not dissipate upon contact with the atmosphere. It converted cleanly into thermal radiation, extreme ionization, and a displaced pillar of compressed gas.

At Point Conception, three hundred road miles west of the valley floor, the Pacific Ocean did not splash; it ionized. At the moment of contact, the shallow continental shelf was instantly evacuated, the water column flashing into a multi-kilometer plume of superheated, supercritical steam that rose forty miles into the upper atmosphere before the bedrock beneath it could even deform.

The shockwave that rippled through the Earth’s mantle arrived at the base of the Sierra Nevada mountains precisely thirteen minutes before the sound wave followed it through the ash-choked air.

In the old logging hills above Pine Ridge, the world simply shuddered—a brief, vertical jar that sheared the roots of the ancient ponderosa pines and sent centuries of granite scree tumbling down the dry ravines.

Marcus Thorne did not stop walking. His fingers were locked around the heavy brass handle of the leather ledger case strapped to his chest. Behind him, Elena was dragging the two-wheeled utility handcart loaded with their remaining tins of oil-sealed rations, her boots digging deep into the slick, gray silt that was already beginning to dust the forest floor.

"That was the primary core," Marcus said, his voice dropping into the thin, static-heavy air. He didn't check a device; he felt the resonance in the bone of his jaw. "The shelf took the strike. The coastal ranges will deflect the initial lateral thermal flash, but the displacement wave will hit the delta gates by noon. The entire San Joaquin basin is about to become a closed inland sea."

"We aren't high enough yet, Marc," Elena gasped, her breath rising in a tight, frosty plume that lingered in the heavy violet twilight. "If the back-surge forces the river channels to invert, the water will pool against the base of these hills. We need another eight hundred feet of elevation before our lungs fail from the superheated sulfur mix."

"We make the Foothill Ridge Gate by dawn," Marcus said, his thumb brushing the knurled edge of his mechanical artillery stopwatch through the canvas fabric of his vest pocket. The hand was still moving—a persistent, mechanical ticking that outlasted every digital clock, every server farm, and every centralized key authority between the two oceans. "Keep the paper dry, El. If the ink runs, we don't have a way to rebuild the names when the mud settles."
