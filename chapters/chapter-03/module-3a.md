# Module 3A: The Delta Back-Surge

The reverse displacement of the Sacramento-San Joaquin River Delta did not begin with a wave profile that could be measured by the automated maritime telemetry buoys near Suisun Bay. The buoys had already gone silent four hours prior, their solar-recharged telemetry batteries refusing to cycle current after the localized ionization cloud over Point Conception scrambled the gate-voltage parameters of their internal lithium chargers.
The back-surge arrived as a thick, hydraulic swelling—a solid wall of black ocean water mixed with pulverized coastal shelf granite and liquid asphalt that entered the mouth of the delta at seventy miles per hour. It forced the fresh water of the San Joaquin River back up its own channel, turning the shallow, cross-hatched sloughs of the valley floor into an inverted drainage basin.
By noon on October 26, the gray ash falling over the foothills of Pine Ridge had changed from a dry, flake-like powder into a cold, greasy sleet that smelled of deep-sea saltpetre and decaying tule reeds.
Marcus Thorne stood on a granite outcrop two hundred feet above The Foothill Ridge Gate, his boots anchored in a fresh drift of volcanic slurry. The leather ledger case was strapped flat to his ribs beneath his heavy canvas coat, its physical weight a constant, reassuring pressure against his breastbone.
Below him, the unpaved logging path cut a sharp, jagged line through the dark ponderosa pines before disappearing into the dense, slate-gray fog layer that had swallowed the valley floor.
"They're coming up from the Fowler lateral," Elena said. She was standing behind the utility handcart, her hands wrapped in rags as she held a pair of mechanical field glasses to her eyes. The glass lenses were pitted, coated in a fine, abrasive film of silica dust that she had to repeatedly clear with the corner of her sleeve. "It’s not an organized evacuation, Marc. They aren't carrying gear. They’re just walking along the high banks of the canal bed because the asphalt on the county roads is blocked solid by dead engines."
Marcus took the field glasses from her hand. He didn't look toward the highway lines. He focused the pitted optics on a line of human shapes moving through the gray gloom three miles below.
They looked like a column of mechanical ants, their heads down, rags wrapped around their mouths to filter out the superheated sulfur mix drifting from the coast. Many of them were still carrying their mobile handsets—the dead blocks of black glass and copper catching the strange, violet luminescence of the upper atmosphere like useless reflective icons.
"The water is pushing through the Mendota gates right now," Marcus said, his voice flat, his teeth clicking together from the damp chill. "The delta islands are breaching from the south up. Within six hours, that column will have nothing behind it but an open, five-hundred-foot-deep sea of gray mud. If they don't clear the ridge gate before the timber tracks wash out, they’ll be trapped against the granite face."
He stepped down from the outcrop, his hand dropping to the heavy brass buckles of his ledger case. He opened the cover to Page Four across the damp timber rail of an old cattle pen. The columns were empty, the clean white surface of the rag paper waiting for the first names of the migration.
"We don't go down to meet them," Marcus muttered, clicking his mechanical pencil to sharpen the lead point. "If we go down into that fog, we get swallowed by a crowd that’s still waiting for a central authority to issue an emergency ration code. We stay on the stone. We establish the validation line here. If they want to cross into the high country, they enter their names into the book, they surrender their dead electronics to the scrap pile for tool iron, and they accept the ledger credit. The paper is the boundary now."
He dipped his head as a sudden, low-frequency rumble vibrated through the granite beneath his boots—the sonic boom of a secondary comet fragment shearing the upper stratosphere over the eastern edge of the valley, casting a sharp, flickering violet shadow across the empty pages of the new world's first true record.
