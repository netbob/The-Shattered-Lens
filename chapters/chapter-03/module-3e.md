# Module 3E: The First Ledger Lock

The closure of the third accounting cycle at the Foothill Ridge Gate did not require a network synchronization broadcast. It was completed at exactly 18:34 PDT when Marcus Thorne drew a double horizontal rule across the bottom of row forty-two on Page Six using his mechanical pencil.
The gray sleet had turned into a thick, crystalline frost that froze to the linen edges of the ledger paper as soon as the book case was unclasped. The ambient light from the stratosphere had deepened into a dark, pulsing indigo—the exact color of an electrical arc viewed through dirty industrial glass.
A line of six hundred and fourteen names now filled the columns. Behind them, the valley floor was gone, replaced by a vast, featureless sheet of black tidewater that had backed up through the delta gates until it lapped against the granite base of the ridge.
"The water has crested at the five-hundred-foot contour line," Elena said, her boots crunching through the freezing volcanic sludge as she stepped back to the table. She dropped a heavy iron padlock onto the pine boards, its mechanical tumbler clicking open with a clean, metallic snap. "The old logging bridge at the lower lateral just tore free from its abutments. Nothing else is coming up from the Clovis road unless they can swim through three miles of liquid silt."
Marcus didn't look toward the drowned basin. He reached into his coat pocket and pulled out his universal mechanical artillery stopwatch, checking the position of the sweeping steel hand before clicking the top plunger down to lock the balance wheel.
"The registration is closed," Marcus said. He carefully laid a thick sheet of oiled parchment over the wet ink of the final names, then swung the heavy leather front cover down until the brass clasps clicked home into their zinc brackets. "From this hour forward, the ledger is locked. No new entries can be authorized without a triple-sign verification from the ridge nodes. If an identity is not recorded inside these six pages, they have no claim to the silo rations or the deep well water."
He slipped the heavy hasp of Elena's padlock through the iron loops welded onto the ledger case's frame, turning the mechanical key until the brass springs caught inside the housing.
"We have our baseline population," Marcus muttered, his hand tracing the cold leather texture of the locked book. "They’ve surrendered their glass; they’ve logged their iron. Now we move them into the old surface shafts before the atmospheric inversion drops the temperature below zero. If the paper stays dry tonight, we have a legal code for the reconstruction when the sky clears."
