# Module 6C: The Terminal Vent

The failure of the auxiliary air exchange line inside the third surface shaft did not begin with a mechanical rotor failure or a severed belt drive. It occurred when the automated back-draft damper—an aluminum shutter assembly counterweighted to open under positive static pressure—became locked solid within its iron frame, welded shut by a four-inch encrustation of sub-zero volcanic frost that had accumulated during the November 10 freeze [4E, 6A].
By 14:00 PDT, the carbon dioxide concentration within the lower tiers of Shaft 3 had risen to two and a half percent by volume, turning the cold, stagnant air into a heavy, narcotic cloud that left the 532 distributed refugees lightheaded and shivering in the dark [6A, PATCH-009].
Marcus Thorne did not leave his field desk in Pod 1 [4A, 6A]. He sat beneath the amber flicker of the kerosene lanterns, his split slate panel resting across his knees [4D, 6A]. Using his mechanical pencil, he drew a single, heavy diagonal rule through the allocation index for Row Twenty-Eight, reducing the daily salt pork distribution for the Shaft 3 barracks down to four ounces per name [4E, 6A].
"They’re blocking the ladder bays, Marc," Elena said, stepping through the low concrete bulkhead [4D, 6A]. She leaned her Winchester rifle against the iron casing of the central desk, her bare fingers raw, the skin split into red fissures from the sub-zero iron exposure of the deep well line [4C, 6A]. "Robert Cross has his pump crew holding the lower landing with iron pry-bars [5D, 6A]. They’ve uncoupled the primary battery plates from the well circuit and dragged them down to the ventilation trunk [5E, 6A]. They’re trying to use the raw current to spark an old industrial fan casing they stripped from the utility decks [6A]."
Marcus did not clear the slate columns [6A]. He picked up his grandfather’s mechanical artillery stopwatch from his waistcoat pocket, clicking the plunger down to halt the sweeping steel hand [4D, 6A]. The crisp, mechanical click echoed hollowly through the unpainted concrete vault [4A, 4D].
"The battery plates stay anchored to the fluid circuit, Elena," Marcus said, his voice flat, carrying the absolute neutrality of an auditor who had outlasted the infrastructure [4E, 6A]. "If they discharge those cells down to ten volts trying to spin a shorted motor, the rotary vanes in the deep well will freeze within six hours [5E, 6A]. If the pump freezes, the Registry line terminates, and those five hundred and thirty-two names drop off the allocation ledger permanently [6A]."
He stood up, his hand clamping down onto the brass padlock of the leather ledger case with an unyielding grip [3E, 4E, 6A]. He didn't take a weapon. He took a piece of white calcium chalk and walked toward the dark mouth of the stairwell where the low, angry hum of the crowd was crawling up from the lower decks [6A].
"You can't freeze us out to protect a book, Thorne," a voice called out from the dark below—Robert Cross, his face drawn, his fingers clamped around the rusted iron frame of his mechanical wrench [5D, 6A]. Behind him, sixty pairs of eyes caught the pale, oily reflection of the kerosene wicks, their faces wrapped in dirty rags to filter out the stale air [3D, 4A]. "The children in Shaft 3 are vomiting from the air rot. Give us the five gallons of winterized diesel for the generator or we split the padlock on that desk ourselves [6A, [ITEM-005]]."
Marcus stopped at the top riser of the concrete stairs. He did not look at the iron wrench. He used his chalk to draw a thick, vertical line straight down the face of the unpainted concrete wall, splitting the entrance bay into two uniform halves.
"The line is the boundary, Mr. Cross," Marcus said, his voice carrying a mechanical cadence that outlasted the network [3D, 6A]. "The five gallons of fuel inside that vault represent forty thousand gallons of clear water drawn from the stone [5E]. If you burn it to spin a dead fan, you don't clear the air; you just ensure that everyone below this ridge dies of dehydration by Friday afternoon [5D, 6A]. Sign your name to the shift rotation for the manual air-bleed crew or I rule a line through your index right now. The paper doesn't adjust for your panic."
The crowd in the stairwell did not surge forward. They looked at Elena’s hand resting near the stock of her rifle, then at the sharp, white chalk line splitting the concrete under their boots [6A]. The absolute silence of the mountain closed back over Pod 1 like ice sealing a well, leaving nothing but the steady, rhythmic click-click-click of the mechanical chronometer tracking the slow, frozen regression of human law [4D, 5D, 6A].
