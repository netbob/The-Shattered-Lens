# Module 6D: The Outer Shear

The physical clearing of the primary intake louvers above Pod 1 did not allow for standard alpine safety gear or heated outerwear. By November 12, 2026, the absolute temperature along the exposed granite ridges of Pine Ridge had plummeted to thirty-eight degrees below zero, driven by an accelerating tropospheric freezing loop that turned the falling volcanic silt into a continuous, razor-sharp sleet of micro-crystalline silica [5D, 6A].
Elena Vance led the four-man manual air-bleed crew out through the concrete escape hatch, her boots wrapped in triple layers of rope to find purchase against the frozen granite slope [5D, 6C]. The wind outside was a sustained fifty-knot horizontal scream that carried no ambient light from the indigo horizon—only the raw, blinding blackness of an atmospheric inversion that had permanently sealed the western hemisphere under an opaque shroud of formaldehyde frost [3C, 6B].
"The survival of Shaft 3 is pinned to this intake line, Cross," Elena shouted into the roar, her lips splitting open beneath her canvas rags as the sub-zero wind dried the moisture on her breath [5B, 6C]. She unbuckled the heavy iron shaft of the two-man crosscut logging saw from the handcart frame [5D, [ITEM-002]]. "The aluminum shutters are jammed under four inches of frozen silt slurry [6A, 6C]. If we don't shear the ice collar away from the bypass hinge with the commercial chisels within twenty minutes, the lower barracks will hit fatal carbon dioxide levels before dawn [6A, 6C]."
Robert Cross and a mechanic from the pumping crew stepped onto the sub-zero casing of the ventilation terminal, their bodies bent at a forty-five-degree angle to resist being thrown off the ridge by the katabatic draft [5D, 6C]. Working by the blind, tactile feedback of their numbed arms, they drove the tips of the eight-inch commercial cold chisels directly into the rock-hard ice encrustation blocking the damper pivot [5D, [ITEM-013]].
Marcus Thorne did not join the ridge team. He remained three floors below, standing inside the dry, concrete access bay of Pod 1 with his grandfather’s mechanical stopwatch ticking against his ribs [4D, 6C]. He held his pencil poised over the split slate panel, tracking the minutes against the crew's estimated oxygen burn rate with the methodical, unyielding focus of a network router calculating packet drop ratios [4D, 6C].
On the mountain crest, Elena brought her weight against the handle of the logging saw, its spring-steel teeth grinding through the dense volcanic ice shelf with a high-pitched, metallic shriek that was instantly swallowed by the gale [5D].
The ice collar cracked—a sharp, mechanical pop of fracturing aggregate that released the tension on the aluminum shutter casing [5E, 6C]. The back-draft damper swung open by six inches, its heavy counterweight plunging into the frozen drift with a dull thud as the low-pressure void inside Pod 1 instantly began drawing the clean, freezing high-altitude air down into the dark, suffocating grid lines of the lower shafts [6A, 6C].
