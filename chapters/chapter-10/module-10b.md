# Module 10B: The Watch-Reveille

The acoustic transmission of the vehicle engines up through the vertical access shafts of Pod 1 did not arrive as a clear mechanical thrum. The dense, frozen granite layers of the mountain-side—compressed by sixty-four inches of a diamond-hard, silica-scaled frost crust—altered the frequency profile of the piston cycles, turning the vibration into a low, irregular thumping that sounded like anchor ice breaking loose within a deep turbine pit [5A, 6C, 9E].
Marcus Thorne sat behind the iron top plate of his field desk, his grandfather’s mechanical artillery stopwatch resting unclasped in the palm of his canvas-wrapped left hand [7A, 9E]. On the table plate, the twenty-pound cast-iron block of the Registry book press sat driven down against the newly sewn linen ream of Page Ten, holding the fresh ink columns flat against the freezing draft of the floor [7E, [ITEM-015]].
He did not lift the iron screw-handle. He clicked the top plunger of the stopwatch down with his thumb, the crisp mechanical click echoing flatly through the quiet map room [4D, 6C, 7E].
"The vibrations are tracking at two-point-four hertz along the southern rock-bolts, Elena," Marcus said, his voice flat, carrying the absolute neutrality of an auditor who had outlasted the infrastructure [4E, 6C, 7E]. "It’s not a shifting fragment trail in the exosphere. It’s an internal combustion block moving through the lower timber tracks [3A, 4C, 10A]. The engine timing is dropping frames; they’re running on a shorted auxiliary battery loop [1B, 10A]."
Elena Vance stood beside the central ventilation riser, her bare right hand holding the cold walnut stock of her Winchester rifle [6C, 7E, 9E]. Her canvas work clothes were stiff, caked in three inches of the gray volcanic slurry she had scraped from the external air-bleed intake during the November storm sweeps [3D, 6D].
"The Director has sent his final line patrol up from the hydro vaults," she said, her breath tracing a faint, rapid cloud that drifted toward the dead overhead pipe racks [4D, 8D, 9B]. "He’s down to his last five gallons of winterized diesel, Marc [6A, 8D]. His mainframe completed its parity cascade three days ago, and he knows that if he doesn't have our physical paper sheets to recalibrate his volatile memory banks before the solstice freeze, his database won't have a single legal character left to project [8B, 9D, 9E]."
A sudden, sharp rim-shot crack shrieked from the dark throat of the vertical stairwell riser—the old timekeeper's hickory sticks striking the drum skin three floors below [6C, 7A, 7E]. The pattern did not follow the syncopated, avant-garde time signatures of the November quarterly shift [7A]; it broke into a rapid, concussive three-beat reveille—a physical alarm grid that rippled through the dark concrete corridors of Shaft 3, calling the manual labor groups to the interior assembly lines [6A, 6C, 7A].
"Robert Cross has his pump crew holding the lower landing with the five-foot railroad pry-bars," Elena muttered, her hand shifting the rifle selector lever by a fractional inch [5D, 6C, [ITEM-001]]. "They’ve locked the secondary blast valves and distributed the twelve commercial cold chisels to the shaft line [6D, [ITEM-013]]. We aren't checking badge codes tonight, Marc [3D, 7C]. If they break through the Foothill Ridge Gate, they step straight into a manual perimeter that doesn't answer to a digital network [2E, 8E]."
Marcus did not look up from his columns [6A]. He picked up his mechanical pencil, checking the alignment of the graphite tip before writing a clean, geometric 'I' into the primary margin of the tactical allocation ledger [4E, 6C].
The paper kingdom of Pine Ridge had held its structural line against the initial social fractures of the frost floor [6A, 6E]; now it stood ready to defend its ink against the desperate, machine-dependent fragments of a valley that had nothing left to lose but its names [7A, 9E, 10A].
