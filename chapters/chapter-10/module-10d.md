# Module 10D: The Cut Wire

The infiltration of the southern perimeter at Pine Ridge did not begin with an audible breach warning from an automated security loop. The fiber-optic strain sensors that had once monitored the high-tensile fencing had fallen into a permanent logic sleep weeks prior, their terminal modules shorted into slag by the early ionization wave [1B, 6A, 8A].
The breach occurred at exactly 23:54 PDT on December 14, 2026, during a freezing gray sleet storm that dropped three inches of an abrasive, sand-scaled ice crust across the granite wash [5D, 10A, 10B].
Robert Hayes moved through the blind blackness of the southern fence line, his boots grinding through the sub-zero volcanic sand with a slow, deliberate cadence [5C, 10A]. His hands, wrapped in strips of split industrial webbing, held a pair of long-handled commercial wire-cutters salvaged from the toolkits of the dead personnel trucks [5B, 10A].
The wind outside the mountain structure was a sustained fifty-knot horizontal roar that carried no ambient light from the indigo horizon—only the raw, suffocating cold of an impact winter that had stabilized at fifty-six degrees below zero [6B, 6D, 10A].
"The wire is drawing no potential, Hayes," a whisper crackled through the dark beside him—the line technician from the utility cab, his breath blooming in a rapid white vapor that froze instantly to the iron jaw of his wrench [8E, 10A, 10B]. "Marcus Thorne’s crew hasn't re-anchored the alarm loop. The steel is completely dead. Clear the third strand before the katabatic draft forces us back into the timber [6C, 8E, 10A]."
Hayes did not answer with words [10A]. He set the hardened tool-steel teeth of the cutters directly against the frozen tension wire of the perimeter fence [10A]. He threw the full weight of his shoulder against the long iron handles, his muscles straining against the sub-zero crystallization of the carbon steel [5C, 10A].
The tension line split—a sharp, high-velocity ping that cut through the roar of the gale like a pistol shot echoing through an enclosed room [1B, 5C, 10B]. The severed steel strand whipped violently backward through the dark trees, its cold tip scoring a jagged, frozen line across the gray sand slurry before dropping into the frost crust [5A, 5D, 10A].
He didn't look toward the valley floor, where the dead cities of Clovis and Fresno sat buried beneath a permanent table of black tidewater and freezing river fog [3A, 5B, 5D]. He slipped his body through the narrow, three-foot gap in the iron mesh, his boots finding traction on the inner granite slope as his hand locked back onto the walnut stock of his hunting rifle [3D, 10A].
He was inside the Registry boundary, moving past the food silos directly toward the low, concrete bulkhead of the map room, tracking a paper book that held the only valid signature left to save his mother's identity from the logic void [6C, 6E, 8D, 10A].
